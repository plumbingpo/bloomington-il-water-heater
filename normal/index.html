Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Normal

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Common Water Heater Problems in Normal

Every type of water heater has its own weak points, and we work on all of them around Normal. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks burn out heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
