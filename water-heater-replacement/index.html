When to Call for Water Heater Replacement

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Rust or corrosion on the tank, fittings, or vent connector
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
