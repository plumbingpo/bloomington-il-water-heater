Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • Hot water far hotter than normal, a sign of a runaway thermostat or gas control
  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life

The Benefits of Professional Emergency Water Heater Repair

The risks in a water heater emergency are easy to underestimate. In reality, you may be dealing with standing water beside 240-volt wiring, a gas supply and live burner a few inches from a puddle, and a tank of very hot water under pressure. The relief valve, if it is firing, is the last safeguard between an overheated tank and a violent failure, and plugging it is one of the most dangerous things anyone can do to a heater. Relighting a pilot while gas lingers, or forcing a rusted connection, can make things much worse. Evacuate for gas, isolate, then phone is the right sequence for anyone without the tools and training.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. You get that answer before any work begins.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Annual flushing keeps sediment from causing the rumbling and hot spots that lead to leaks
  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Lift the relief valve lever once a year to confirm it opens and reseats, and call if it keeps dripping
  • A 120F setting is safer for skin and easier on the heater than the hottest setting
