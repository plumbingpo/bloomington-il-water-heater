Most gas water heaters in Bloomington, IL work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve shuts off the gas for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across Bloomington, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Safety always comes first: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and Bloomington Water Heater can take over from there.

Once we know the cause, the repair is usually simple. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob
  • A flame that looks orange or floppy rather than steady and blue
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater

Why Professional Gas Water Heater Services Matters

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. A pilot outage might come from a worn thermocouple, a partly blocked pilot tube, lint on the flame arrestor screen, a downdraft, or an internal valve failure. A wrong guess there just wastes money. Guess wrong on a gas fitting or vent connection and the stakes are much higher, since a small leak or a spillage problem can put gas or carbon monoxide into the living space. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically call for a permit.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
